Why Stopping Cod Fishing Was Not Enough—and How Ecosystem Rebalancing Unlocks Recovery

In 1992, Canada closed one of the world’s largest groundfish fisheries after Northwest Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua) collapsed by over 95%. Yet for more than three decades, cod failed to rebuild. Size-spectrum ecosystem modelling reveals why: removing adult cod triggered an eruption of pelagic forage fishes and benthic mesopredators that prey on cod eggs, larvae, and juveniles—locking the ecosystem into an alternative stable state.

1. The Management Puzzle

Role Reversal on the Shelf: From Cultivator to Prey

Traditional single-species stock assessments assume that setting directed fishing mortality to zero (Fcod = 0) guarantees stock rebuilding. In size-structured marine food webs, however, trophic roles reverse across life stages.

Pre-1975: Cultivated Regime

Adult Cod Protect Their Young

Large adult cod (2–18 kg) consume vast quantities of capelin, herring, mackerel, and sculpin. By keeping these small-bodied species in check, adult cod inadvertently “cultivate” a safe, food-rich nursery for their own 1 mg–20 g eggs, larvae, and newly settled juveniles.

1970–1992: Trawl Truncation

Predator Release & Eruption

Intense groundfish trawling removed large cod faster than recruits could replace them. Freed from top-down predation, planktivorous pelagics (herring, mackerel, capelin) and benthic sculpins surged across the Northwest Atlantic shelf.

1992–Present: The Moratorium Trap

Predator Pit & Bottleneck

Abundant herring and mackerel consume pelagic cod eggs and larvae while competing for zooplankton; abundant sculpins ambush settled 0-group cod. Juvenile survival drops below replacement, trapping cod at 3–5% of historical biomass even at Fcod = 0.

4. Alternative Stable States & Hysteresis

Why Zero Fishing Cannot Cross an Ecological Fold Bifurcation

Equilibrium continuation (scanModel & Newton–Raphson stability analysis) proves that both the high-cod cultivated regime and the low-cod forage-dominated regime are locally stable fixed points (leading eigenvalue Re(λ) < 0) at identical biological parameters and Fcod = 0.

A. Collapse & Hysteresis Loop vs. Directed Cod Fishing (Fcod)

Once historical fishing exceeds the collapse tipping point, the stock drops to the lower stable branch. Reducing Fcod back to zero moves horizontally along the collapsed branch without jumping up.

B. Escape Bifurcation vs. Pelagic Forage Harvest (Fpelagic at Fcod = 0)

Starting on the collapsed moratorium branch, increasing pelagic harvest thins larval predators until the lower branch terminates at the recovery threshold—triggering self-sustaining cod rebuilding.

5. Implementation Guidance for Fisheries Policy Makers

Phased Ecosystem Rebalancing Roadmap & Ecological Guardrails

Crucially, elevated forage fishing does not need to be permanent. Because the high-cod regime is self-stabilising once adult cod biomass re-establishes top-down cultivation, forage harvest is required only as a temporary transitional bridge.

Phase I · Years 1–12

Targeted Trophic Release

Maintain strict cod bycatch caps (Fcod ≤ 0.02 /yr) while expanding selective pelagic harvest on abundant herring and mackerel stocks and incentivising sculpin trap/pot removals in key juvenile cod nursery bays.

Phase II · Years 12–20

Cultivation Hand-Off

Monitor 0-group cod survival, juvenile feeding levels, and spawning biomass crossing the Allee cultivation threshold. As rebuilding adult cod resume natural predation on forage fish, taper pelagic harvest quotas downward to prevent combined over-depletion.

Phase III · Year 20+

Balanced Multispecies Fishery

Return pelagic harvest to precautionary baseline rates and reopen a size-selective, moderate cod fishery (Fcod ≈ 0.15–0.18 /yr, L50 ≥ 52 cm) safely below the hysteresis collapse threshold.

Ecological Safeguards & Precautionary Guardrails: Forage fishes such as capelin and herring also support seabirds, baleen whales, and pinnipeds. Our size-spectrum results show that indiscriminate depletion of capelin is neither necessary nor desirable: selective harvest focused on abundant ichthyoplankton predators (adult herring and mackerel during spawning overlap) paired with adaptive step-down triggers (maintaining forage biomass above 40% of unexploited capacity) achieves cod recovery while preserving marine predator forage bases and avoiding cod bycatch via midwater/purse-seine gears.
